The paper's practical selling point is that its new estimator measures an LLM's benchmark accuracy more precisely than a simple average at the same evaluation cost, and its Tables 1-3 report exactly that across 60 model-benchmark cells, landing "significantly closer to the ground truth" every time, without a single uncertainty interval anywhere in the paper ("bootstrap" and "standard error" appear zero times). Re-running the authors' own unmodified code, that claim does not survive: 58 of 60 of their reported gains are smaller than one standard error of ordinary sampling noise at the sample sizes they used, three cells contradict their own rows, and at low model noise the estimator is measurably WORSE than the average it replaces, carrying 33% more variance (95% CI [-0.495,-0.185], 250 repetitions, with a positive control passing at the paper's own default setting). What is true instead is the weaker claim their own Corollary 4.7 already predicts, a small improvement that is real on average but invisible in any single cell: 27 of 30 positive once cells sharing an evaluation subset are grouped (p=4.2e-06), one cell genuinely clearing a no-signal null (+3.50%), and the same picture on a live GSM8K run with open-weight models (AUROC 0.73, 0.80), where the average gain is +0.24pp but one evaluation run swings anywhere from -2.97 to +3.63. That is why it fits this award: a headline claim fails, a weaker and better-supported one takes its place, and every number here re-derives from the published data in one second via code/verify_headlines.py.
