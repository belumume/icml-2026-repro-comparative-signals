ORIGINAL CLAIM. Remark 4.8 promises an assured efficiency gain in deployment ("ensuring efficiency gain"), and Tables 1-3 report the one-step estimator landing "significantly closer to the ground truth" than the naive mean, with gains of 0.24%-12.00% across 60 cells.

EVIDENCE AGAINST IT. (1) On the authors' unmodified simulation, at sigma=0.08 the estimator carries 33% MORE variance than the mean it replaces, 95% CI [-0.495,-0.185] over 250 replications; positive control passes at their default sigma=1.0. (2) No gain carries an interval: "bootstrap" and "standard error" occur zero times in the paper. Recomputed in binomial-noise units at the reported N, 58 of 60 gains fall under 1.0 SE (57 as printed), median 0.41 SE, and that marginal SE is an upper bound because Improv is paired. (3) Three cells contradict their own row, one printing +0.50% where both estimates are identical to the digit (97.00). Against our own case: one cell does clear a no-signal null (+3.50%, GSM8K), fewer than the three chance predicts, and the logbook reports it.

WHAT IS TRUE INSTEAD. A small systematic absolute-error reduction, real in aggregate and unresolvable per cell, which is what their Corollary 4.7 already predicts. Clustering to one value per benchmark-model pair (the configs share an evaluation subset) gives 27 of 30 positive, exact binomial p=4.2e-06. A live GSM8K run on open-weight models agrees: mean +0.24pp, 95% CI of the mean [+0.167,+0.312]; a single run spans [-2.97,+3.63].
