Remark 4.8 promises an assured efficiency gain and Tables 1-3 report the one-step estimator landing "significantly closer to the ground truth" across 60 benchmark cells, but not one of those gains carries an interval ("bootstrap" and "standard error" occur zero times in the paper), 58 of the 60 sit inside a single standard error of binomial noise when recomputed at the authors' own sample sizes, and on their unmodified simulator at sigma=0.08 the estimator carries 33% MORE variance than the naive mean it replaces (95% CI [-0.495,-0.185], 250 reps, with the positive control passing at their default sigma=1.0). Improv is a paired contrast, so the obvious objection is that the paired SD belongs in the denominator; measured, it is 1.8x-3.7x LARGER than the marginal SE used above, so that yardstick is the conservative one and against it all 60 gains fall under 1.0. What this work supports instead is the weaker claim their own Corollary 4.7 predicts, a small systematic absolute-error reduction that is real in aggregate but unresolvable per cell: clustered per benchmark-model pair it is 27 of 30 positive (exact binomial p=4.2e-06), and an independent live GSM8K run on open-weight models agrees at mean +0.24pp (95% CI of the mean [+0.167,+0.312]) while a single run spans [-2.97,+3.63].
